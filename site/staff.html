How the numbers work

  • A full-time position (the City calls it a full-time equivalent, or FTE) is 35 to 40 hours of work a week. One person or several part-time people can fill it, so these are positions, not a count of people. The annual reports give the same counts for 2023 to 2025 as the budget does, so they appear to be budgeted positions rather than the number of people on payroll.
  • "City" covers every City department, including the Calgary Police Service, council offices and the City Auditor. It leaves out ENMAX. Related authorities are organizations the City controls but runs at arm's length, such as the Calgary Public Library, Calgary Economic Development, Calgary Municipal Land Corporation and Calgary Arts Development. Annual reports before 2019 gave one total for both, so years before 2015 can't be split.
  • Payroll is "salaries, wages and benefits" in the City's consolidated financial statements. It covers the City and its related authorities, everyone paid (including temporary and seasonal staff and overtime), and benefits such as pension contributions. Payroll per position is payroll divided by positions, so it's a rough average, not anyone's salary.
  • Annual reports sometimes restate earlier years. Each year here uses the figure from the newest report that has it.
  • Department and business unit figures come from the budget, which starts at 2023 because the City reorganized its departments in 2022. The City rounds each line, so business units can differ from their department's total by a position or two.
  • "City administration" is every department except the Police Service, Council, Civic Partners and Corporate Programs. Its positions are split into management exempt (managers and other staff outside a union) and union and other. The budget shows about 490 fewer management exempt and 1,370 more union positions in 2025 without saying why.
  • The City's own workforce page counts City administration only (no police, council, Civic Partners, on-call staff or students): 12.9 positions per 1,000 people on November 30, 2024, down from 14 ten years earlier.
  • The proposed 2027–2030 budget is due on November 10, 2026. The daily collection checks the budget pages and will add it when it's published.

Data: City of Calgary Annual Financial Reports 2010 to 2025 and the 2026 budget as approved. Code: github.com/TrackingOurCash/YYC.
